Filming on a DJI Osmo Action?

  1. Record the ride on your watch, bike computer or an app like Strava, and film as usual.
  2. Get the clip onto your phone with the DJI Mimo app or a card reader. Mimo also keeps the camera's clock right.
  3. Export the ride as GPX or FIT from Strava, Garmin Connect, Komoot or your watch's app.
  4. Open both in Overlayer. They're matched by recording time, so your speed shows at exactly the right moment.

Using the DJI GPS Bluetooth Remote? Reading its data straight from the clip is on the way. For now, use your watch or phone's ride file.

Your rides stay on your phone.

Overlayer has no internet permission, so it can't send anything anywhere. Your location history, heart rate and videos never leave your phone. No accounts, ads, analytics or tracking.

Good to know

Which cameras work?

Any camera that records MP4 or MOV: every DJI Osmo Action (including the Action 5 Pro and Action 4), Insta360, GoPro, drones and phones. Pair the video with a GPX or FIT file from your watch, bike computer or sports app. DJI drones and GoPro clips carry their own data, so they need no extra file. See the DJI Osmo Action steps.

How do I use my Strava activity?

On the Strava website, open the activity, choose the ⋯ menu and Export GPX. Open that file in Overlayer and pick your video.

When is it coming out?

Android is first and is in testing now. The iPhone version is in development. Leave your email below to hear the day each one launches, or to join the Android beta.

Which phones does it run on?

Android 8.0 or newer. Exporting in 4K needs a phone that can record 4K itself; most phones from the last few years can.

Does it need an internet connection?

Never. Everything happens on your phone, so it works on a mountain with no signal just as well as at home.

Are the numbers accurate?

They're exactly what your devices recorded, lightly smoothed to look good on video. GPS and sensors aren't perfect, so treat the overlay as a great-looking record, not an official timing or medical tool.
